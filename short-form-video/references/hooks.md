# Hook patterns

The pattern library behind the hook step in [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md). Each pattern is a **skeleton**: fill the brackets from the topic, then cut the line down to the hook bar in `SKILL.md`, which is five to eight spoken words carried by the overlay and the opening frame. Every example is written in Chet's counter register.

Adapted from a creator hook bank (Hitthefyp.com, "100 Hooks"). This file keeps the patterns that suit a licensed pharmacist on camera and rewrites them. It is not a copy of the source.

## Proven: reach for these first

These four were marked as the ones that work. Draft at least one of the three hooks from this set.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| I'm about to show you exactly how [to solve a common problem]. | "I'm about to show you exactly how to split a pill." |
| How do you [get past the thing holding them back]? | "How do you remember which pills you already took?" |
| So this just happened at [the counter / the store]. | "So this just happened at the pharmacy counter." |
| So I have a theory on why [something in their world] is about to [change]. | "So I have a theory on why your pharmacy line is longer this winter." |

"So this just happened" opens a story beat. The story is held to the de-identification rule in `SKILL.md`: it describes a scenario the pharmacy sees often, not an individual a local viewer could recognize.

## Problem-first

Name a problem the viewer already has, then promise the fix.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Here's one simple trick to [avoid problem A] or [problem B]. | "One simple trick to never miss a dose." |
| If you ever [hit this problem], do this right away. | "If a new antibiotic gives you a rash, do this." |
| Here's what to do when you're [state] but only have [short time]. | "Sick, and the doctor can't see you until Monday?" |
| Save this for the next time you need to [action]. | "Save this for your next cold." |
| The number-one thing to do when [frustration blocks them]. | "What to do when your prescription gets denied." |
| [Number] facts that can save you [resource] when you need it most. | "Two facts that save you money at the counter." |
| Here's what to do at home if you want your [person] to [outcome]. | "Parents, do this before you give kids cough syrup." |

## Polarizing

Correct a habit the viewer holds, delivered as a favor rather than a scolding. House tone is service, so the polarizing hooks in this library correct a habit gently and never accuse the viewer.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Not every [category] is [what they assume]. | "Not every vitamin on that shelf is worth buying." |
| Avoid these [two] [habits] if you're trying to [goal]. | "Two things to avoid if you take a blood thinner." |
| Things you're probably doing that you shouldn't: [quick list]. | "Three things you're doing with your medicine cabinet." |
| Here's a reminder to stop [common habit]. | "Stop keeping your pills in the bathroom." |
| Before you [common action], do this. | "Before you crush a pill, check this." |

## Curiosity gap

Hold back the payoff, so the viewer stays to get it.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Wish I'd known this earlier. | "Wish I'd known this earlier: you can ask for 90 days." |
| Please tell me I'm not the only one who just found out [trick]. | "Am I the only one who didn't know refills can sync?" |
| Did you know you can tell [something] just by [an odd detail]? | "Did you know the line on a pill means something?" |
| I bet you've never heard this before. | "I bet nobody's told you this about generics." |
| Here's something I tell [people], and they never believe me. | "Here's something patients never believe when I tell them." |
| I learned this in [setting], and almost nobody talks about it. | "I learned this in pharmacy school. Nobody talks about it." |
| If I could only keep one [item], it'd be [X]. | "If I could keep one thing in my medicine cabinet..." |

## Insider

Built on the presenter's credential: the hook works because a pharmacist is the one saying it. Pair these hooks with a Who beat that delivers the real credential.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| [Number] questions [profession] get asked all the time. | "Three questions pharmacists get every single day." |
| Everybody asks me "[question]?" Here's my answer. | "Everybody asks me if generics are really the same." |
| Here's how I [do X] as a [professional]. | "How I sort my own parents' pills, as a pharmacist." |
| [Number] things to ask [person] before [decision]. | "Three questions to ask before starting a new medication." |
| The tip I wish every [audience] knew on day one. | "The tip I give every new caregiver." |
| Here's a sentence I hear at the counter every week. | "Here's a sentence I hear at the counter every week." |
| A day in my life as a [job] in [place]. | "A day in my life as a pharmacist in Lititz." |

## Series

Turns one topic file into repeat episodes, and a high part number signals a running show worth following. Map each part to a topic-library entry (`Topic X video Y`).

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Things every [audience] should know, part [N]. | "Things every caregiver should know, part 3." |
| [Number] questions I ask every [audience] on day one. | "Three questions I ask every new patient." |
| Out of everything I've shared, these are my top [N]. | "My top five tips from this year, in one video." |

## Update

Rides a change in the viewer's world. Every update hook carries a moving fact, so its claim goes on the shoot sheet's **Verify before filming** list.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Stop what you're doing, [thing they use] just changed. | "Medicare Part D just changed. Here's what matters." |
| Something changed in [space] this week that's a big deal. | "Something changed with cold medicine this year." |
| Here's another update for [audience] you're going to like. | "Good news if you're on Medicare." |

## Left out of the source

Several families from the source bank stay out because they cost a pharmacist trust: celebrity and trending-gossip hooks, "they don't want me to show you this" secrecy, outcome guarantees ("lose 20 pounds in 30 days", "change your life"), hot takes on relationships or politics, and profanity. When a user asks for one of these, offer the nearest pattern above.
